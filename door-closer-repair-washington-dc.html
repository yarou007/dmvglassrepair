Door Closer Problems We Assess

Closer symptoms can come from the hydraulic body, arm, mounting, door alignment, hinges or pivots, latch, or more than one part of the entrance.

Leaking Closer Body

Visible fluid, residue, or loss of controlled movement around the closer body or nearby door surface.

Slamming or Slow Closing

A door that closes too quickly, moves inconsistently, or takes too long to reach the frame.

Closing Without Latching

A door that reaches the frame but remains open because speed, alignment, strike, or latch behavior is not working together.

Closer Body, Arm, Mounting, and Adjustment

Closer Body

The body contains the mechanism that controls the swing. Leakage, wear, or internal faults may change how the door closes.

Arm and Mounting

Loose, bent, damaged, or incorrectly positioned arm and mounting components can change force and movement.

Adjustment or Replacement

Adjustment is only one possible path. A leaking, worn, damaged, or mismatched closer may need repair or replacement instead.

What to Tell Us When You Call

A specific description helps identify what should be checked at the door and whether the issue appears limited to the closer.

  • The property location and whether the door serves retail, restaurant, office, apartment, or mixed-use space
  • The door material and whether the closer is surface-mounted, concealed, overhead, or floor-mounted if known
  • Whether you see fluid, a loose arm, damaged mounting, or another visible change
  • Whether the door slams, closes slowly, stays open, or reaches the frame without latching
  • Whether wind, weather, or heavy building traffic changes the symptom

What Happens During the Assessment

1. Review the Door Swing

Closing speed, sweep, final approach, latching, opening force, and the reported symptom are observed.

2. Check the Connected Parts

The closer body, arm, mounting, door alignment, hinges or pivots, strike, and latch are reviewed as relevant.

3. Discuss the Service Path

Adjustment, repair, or replacement options are based on the source of the problem and the condition of the components.

How Closer Problems Affect Building Use

A closer issue changes how customers, staff, tenants, residents, and delivery personnel move through an entrance. Slamming can make the door difficult to use, slow closing can interrupt traffic, and non-latching can prevent the door from returning to its normal closed position.

  • Retail and restaurant entrances with frequent customer and delivery use
  • Office and mixed-use doors shared throughout the workday
  • Apartment and managed-property common doors with repeated daily cycles

Frequently Asked Questions

What door closer problems do you assess?

We assess leaking closer bodies, loose or damaged arms, slamming, slow closing, doors that stay open, and doors that reach the frame but do not latch.

Can a door closer be adjusted, or does it need replacement?

That depends on the closer body, arm, mounting, door alignment, and the source of the fault. Adjustment is not appropriate for every leak, damaged component, or worn closer.

What information should I provide when I call?

Tell us the property location, door type, closer position, the symptom you observe, whether the door latches, and how building traffic or weather affects the problem.

What happens during a door closer assessment?

The door swing, closer body, arm, mounting, alignment, hinges or pivots, and latch behavior are reviewed before adjustment, repair, or replacement options are discussed.
