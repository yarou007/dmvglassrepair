Emergency Board-Up Service: When Your Business Needs It

Emergency board-up is not just for major disasters. It is often the fastest way to protect a business entrance, storefront, or window after unexpected breakage.

When Board-Up Is the Right Immediate Step

Board-up is appropriate when broken glass or door damage leaves an opening exposed, cannot be locked, contains unstable edges, or cannot receive permanent replacement during the first visit. It creates temporary separation while the opening is assessed and replacement is planned.

Common Situations That May Require Secure-Up

  • A shattered storefront pane after impact, vandalism, or a break-in.
  • Broken entry-door glass that prevents the entrance from being secured.
  • A damaged window, sidelite, or transom with loose or missing glass.
  • A storm- or accident-damaged opening exposed to weather or public access.
  • An opening that needs custom glass not available for immediate installation.

What Board-Up Does—and What It Does Not Do

Board-up is a temporary secure-up measure, not the finished glass repair. Its purpose is to cover the damaged opening while the pane, frame, door, and surrounding conditions are measured and the correct permanent materials are prepared.

1. Secure the Opening

The damaged area is isolated and covered when needed to reduce access through the exposed opening.

2. Assess and Measure

The glass location, dimensions, frame condition, door operation, and existing glass type are reviewed.

3. Coordinate Replacement

The next step is planned for the correct glass or for related frame and door work identified during assessment.

What to Explain During the Call

Provide the business address, the type and location of the opening, whether the property can still be locked, whether glass is loose or missing, and how the site can be accessed. If the broken opening is part of a storefront, the storefront glass repair process may follow the temporary board-up.
