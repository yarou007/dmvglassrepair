How Property Managers Should Handle Broken Commercial Glass

When commercial glass breaks in a managed property, a clear safety, access, approval, and tenant-communication workflow helps the team move from incident report to secure-up and permanent replacement.

Build a First-Response Workflow

Protect people, isolate the damaged area, record the incident, and determine whether the opening is exposed. Then identify the property contact, tenant access limits, and approval path before service is dispatched.

Information to Collect Before Dispatch

A complete phone intake reduces follow-up and helps separate emergency secure-up from scheduled replacement.

  • Property address, building name, suite or tenant, and onsite contact.
  • Opening location: storefront, door, office window, lobby, or interior area.
  • Current condition: cracked, shattered, missing, fogged, or unsecured.
  • Tenant hours, alarm or security contacts, and occupied-area restrictions.
  • Dock, elevator, loading-zone, parking, and after-hours access notes.
  • Approval contact, purchase-order process, and required service documentation.

How to Reduce Approval Delays

Use a defined escalation contact, a simple opening-by-opening scope, and clear access instructions. Keep urgent secure-up separate from the permanent glass decision so each approval is understandable.

Organize Multi-Site Requests by Property

Use a separate address, opening description, access contact, approval contact, and status for each site. This prevents an urgent exposed opening at one property from being mixed with scheduled glass work at another.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the first step after tenant-reported glass damage?

Protect people, isolate the area, document damage, and dispatch secure-up if the opening is exposed.

What documentation should property managers keep?

Keep incident notes, tenant communication records, approval details, the service scope, and final closeout confirmation.

How should teams organize service across multiple sites?

Use one escalation contact, standardized scope notes, and clear access instructions for each property.
